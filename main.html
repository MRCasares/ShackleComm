Earth-Moon Orbital Mechanics, Data & Relay Satellites

1. 3D Earth-Moon Orbital Frame & Tidal Locking

• Geocentric Ecliptic Frame: Earth sits at the scene origin with its 23.44° axial obliquity and UTC sidereal rotation, while the Sun illuminates the Earth-Moon system along the true ecliptic solar vector.
• Moon Orbit & Tidal Locking: The Moon revolves around Earth along its 5.145° inclined orbit (27.32d sidereal / 29.53d synodic phase cycle). Its MOON_ME prime meridian (0° Lon) remains tidally locked toward Earth while exhibiting real optical libration (±7.6° longitude, ±6.68° latitude), producing authentic lunar phases as you scrub or press Play.

2. NASA Lunar Datasets & Airless Horizon Model

• Surface Imagery & Sites: NASA SVS LRO/LOLA lroc_color_2k.jpg and IAU MOON_ME coordinates for NASA Artemis III candidate sites, LOLA South Pole catalogs, and Apollo/CLPS/Chandrayaan/Chang’e missions.
• Airless Line-of-Sight: Visibility uses a 72-sector (5°) approximate terrain horizon based on synthetic topography. It is NOT a loaded NASA LOLA elevation grid. Solar irradiance is computed for a horizontal surface as 1361 × max(0, sin(solar elevation)) W/m² when the Sun clears the horizon; tilted sun-tracking arrays receive a different flux.

3. Three DSN Ground Station Links

The Madrid (Spain), Goldstone (USA), and Canberra (Australia) complexes are Earth-fixed at their geographic coordinates, rotating only with Earth. Each direct line terminates at its station rather than at Earth's center. The lunar relay orbital planes are modeled in Moon-centered ICRF coordinates, not in the rotating lunar surface frame. Each orbiting relay also checks an independent satellite-to-station straight line against Earth and Moon occultation; a lunar-base-to-relay hop counts as an Earth connection only if it has at least one such downlink. The main Earth Connection status combines direct base-to-DSN and base-to-relay-to-DSN paths; DTE refers only to the former. The complexes are checked independently. A direct link exists only when Earth clears the selected lunar location's modeled terrain horizon and the Moon is above that DSN station's geometric horizon at the simulated UTC time. Link beams and connection statistics use this same rule. Ground-station availability, actual antenna pointing, weather, terrain at the station, radio link budget, and mission scheduling are not modeled. Lunar positions use a low-precision analytical ephemeris.

4. NASA LCRNS 3.1 Proposed Reference Constellation

• NASA government reference, NOT launched or operational: The five proposed vehicles SV-1 to SV-5 are initialized from NASA LCRNS Reference Constellation 3.1, Table 2 (Moon-centered ICRF position km and velocity km/s) at 2027-03-01 00:00 UTC. The exact Table 1 Keplerian elements (Moon Principal Axis frame) appear below. The spacecraft have ~30-hour ELFO periods.
• Propagation accuracy: The interactive HTML derives an inertial two-body Keplerian orbit from each NASA state. It does not integrate NASA's recommended DE421 / lunar GRGM-900c 25×25 gravity / Sun + Venus + Earth + Mars + Jupiter + Saturn / solar radiation pressure model. Positions away from the reference epoch and all relay link availability are illustrative predictions, not NASA-validated ephemerides. The lunar orientation, terrain horizon, and DSN geometry are also approximate.
• Visual scale: Satellite altitudes and Earth–Moon separation use deliberately different drawing scales; all sphere occultation and radio-horizon checks run in physical kilometres. The assumed 5° lunar terminal mask is not an orbital parameter given in the NASA paper.
